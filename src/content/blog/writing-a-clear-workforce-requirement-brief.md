Most recruitment problems can be traced back to an unclear brief. When the requirement is vague, candidates are selected on assumptions and everyone's time is spent correcting course. A short, well-structured brief avoids that.

## What to include

- **Roles and numbers.** Which positions you need to fill and how many of each.
- **Duties.** What the person will actually do day to day.
- **Skills and experience.** What is essential, and what is simply desirable.
- **Working conditions.** Location, schedule, shift patterns and any physical demands.
- **Timeline.** When you need people in place, and whether that date is fixed or flexible.
- **Terms.** The employment arrangements you can offer, as far as they are decided.

## Be honest about the work

It is tempting to describe a role in the most attractive terms. In practice, candidates who understand the realities of the job from the beginning are more likely to be a good match and to remain in the role.

## Flag what is still undecided

A brief does not need to be perfect. If some decisions are still open, say so. A good recruitment partner will help you work through them during planning rather than discovering them halfway through the search.

## Treat the brief as a working document

As the search progresses and you learn more about the candidate pool, the brief may need adjusting. Keep it current, and share changes promptly so that sourcing stays aligned with what you actually need.
